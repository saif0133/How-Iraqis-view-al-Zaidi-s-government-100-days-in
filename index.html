Survey overview

Strong early approval, but the harder test is whether confidence becomes lasting trust.

The first 100 days show high satisfaction with Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi and relatively strong confidence in his ability to carry the office. The same survey, however, shows persistent pressure from corruption, economic concerns, and uneven service delivery.

What the full set says

The honeymoon is real, but it is not uniform.

Leadership ratings are substantially stronger than ratings for several state institutions. Security performs better than many service sectors, while Kurdish respondents often register lower confidence than Sunni, Shia, or mixed respondents. The result is a government with a strong opening position but clear delivery risks.

71% think the al-Zaidi government will complete its constitutional four-year term.
Source: Independent Institute for Administration and Civil Society Studies (IIACSS), published by the Atlantic Council on October 5, 2026.
